# Creative Fabrica Studio Partnership Concepts
## Claude Design rebuild brief v2, final

This brief supersedes every earlier brief.

### What went wrong in the current version

The current version compresses each idea into a campaign slogan and explains the actual idea several lines later, or not at all.

"Bring Your Make to Life" forces the reader to decode what "make" means before they know what the idea is.

Pages 5 and 6 read like a partnerships operating manual, with process vocabulary that buries the creative thinking.

The document should make a reader think:

"This person understands how consumer partnerships could work here."

Not:

"This person has produced a structured strategy document."

This brief fixes that.

It is a copy and hierarchy rebuild, not a styling pass. The visual system, warmth and restraint of the current version can stay. The words and page structure change as written below.

---

## 1. Two rules that govern everything

### Rule 1: Brutally literal headlines first

Every headline states what the idea actually is, in plain words a first time reader understands instantly.

Campaign name slogans are never headlines.

If a working title genuinely helps, it may appear once, small, inside a concept page. For example:

Working title: Bring Your Make to Life

When in doubt, leave it out.

### Rule 2: Every concept page uses the same hierarchy

Every concept page follows this exact logic and order:

1. THE IDEA
2. WHAT THE USER DOES
3. WHY THEY WOULD CARE
4. WHY THE PARTNER CARES
5. WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES
6. WHO I WOULD TRY

Nothing else gets its own section.

Operational detail such as tracking, moderation, campaign kits or checklists appears only as a single short line where it earns its place, never as a block.

### PAGE 2

Turn what you made into a video

THE IDEA

When a new video model launches in Studio, run a two week challenge around it.

Upload a photo of something you made, the model animates it, then share the original beside the result.

WHAT THE USER DOES

Uploads a photo of a crochet toy, an illustration, a craft or a product they sell.

Studio animates it with the newly launched model.

They post the before and after.

Keep the existing three step visual if it fits:

Upload → Animate → Share

It supports the explanation rather than replacing it.

WHY THEY WOULD CARE

A knitted dragon breathes fire.

A hand drawn character starts moving.

A seller's static product photo becomes a short promotional clip for their shop.

The thing they already love gets a second life.

WHY THE PARTNER CARES

Every platform gets the same model and can publish the same polished AI demo clips.

Creative Fabrica has something most AI platforms do not: a large audience already making, designing and selling real things.

That gives the model partner launch content that looks nothing like another polished AI demo, at exactly the moment it wants people trying the model.

WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES

Entering needs an account and a first generation, so every participant is a signup plus an activation, tracked through one landing page.

The challenge kit is built once and can be rerun when another relevant model launches.

WHO I WOULD TRY

Kling first, if this capability is coming to Studio. This is a potential test only, nothing announced.

Google and ByteDance as the next routes when relevant models become available.

### PAGE 3

Turn your kid's drawing into something real

THE IDEA

A parent uploads a child's drawing.

Studio animates it while keeping the child's lines recognisable, or turns it into a colouring page, a printable keepsake or a craft the family can make.

HERO VISUAL

Keep the hero visual from the current concept, but make it substantially larger:

One LARGE original drawing panel → One LARGE Studio result panel

If real images are supplied, use them.

If real images are not supplied, keep the areas as large editable image frames and state this in the handback note.

Never fill these frames with stock imagery or generated placeholder artwork.

WHAT THE USER DOES

Takes a photo of the drawing, uploads it, picks what it becomes, then shares the original drawing next to the result.

WHY THEY WOULD CARE

The drawing already matters to the family.

The child stays visibly the author. Studio adds motion or turns the drawing into something the family can keep or make without replacing the child's work.

"My five year old designed this" gives the result an immediate story worth sharing.

WHY THE PARTNER CARES

Crayola can extend from art materials into AI assisted creativity.

HEMA gets a practical Dutch family campaign that fits naturally with craft, gifting and printing.

IKEA has previously turned children's drawings into physical products. This is precedent only, not a current programme or proposed partnership.

WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES

Family signups from beyond the current audience, a naturally shareable before and after, and an AI story where the child's creativity remains visibly at the centre.

WHO I WOULD TRY

HEMA for a fast local pilot.

Crayola for the strategic version.

IKEA as the longer route.

One safety line only, styled small:

Any child focused version needs parental consent, careful data handling and human moderation.

### PAGE 4

Let fans create with official brand assets

THE IDEA

A brand provides an approved pack of characters, artwork, symbols or typography.

Creative Fabrica turns those assets into Studio templates.

Fans make their own graphics or short videos with them.

The brand can feature the strongest work.

WHAT THE USER DOES

Picks a template, creates with real official assets from a brand they love, enters the challenge and shares the result.

WHY THEY WOULD CARE

They get to make something using the real assets rather than fan art knockoffs, with the possibility of being featured by the brand itself.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLES

These examples should carry significant visual weight on the page.

Clearly label the entire group:

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLES

Fanta at Halloween

Short haunted animations or party graphics made from an approved Fanta asset pack.

Dune around an appropriate release window

Fan hype graphics or short videos using approved symbols and typography, never actor likenesses.

Call of Duty around a launch

Squad intro reels or gaming graphics made from a licensed emblem and asset pack.

These are illustrative activation routes only, not proposed or active partnerships.

WHY THE PARTNER CARES

Fans spend time creating with the brand instead of only seeing an ad.

The campaign also produces a stream of permissioned fan work that the brand can choose to amplify.

Approved asset packs are the reason a brand's legal team can say yes.

WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES

Every contestant is a signed up user, and each new brand becomes a configuration of the same basic challenge mechanic rather than requiring an entirely new product.

WHO I WOULD TRY

Fanta for a seasonal consumer test.

Warner Bros where an appropriate franchise window exists.

Activision as a higher upside route where the longer licensing cycle is justified.

These are illustrative targets, not proposed or active partnerships.

Crayola for scale.

IKEA for the long game.

BRAND CHALLENGES

Start with consumer brands where participation feels natural and rights are manageable.

Entertainment and gaming offer higher upside, but licensing makes them slower.

Before contacting anyone, answer four questions:

1. What will the user actually make?
2. Why would the partner want to distribute it?
3. What does Creative Fabrica gain?
4. What result can we measure quickly?

If one answer is weak, keep researching. Do not reach out.

Closing line:

Each mechanic can be tested first on Creative Fabrica's own audience, so evidence starts arriving before any partner signs anything.

## 4. Visual hierarchy

Preserve the strongest parts of the current visual system: warmth, restraint, generous spacing and editorial quality.

Do not let design obscure meaning.

The hierarchy should be obvious at normal laptop zoom:

Literal page headline → section label → core thought → supporting detail

The concept itself should always be visually dominant.

Do not turn section labels into decorative objects.

Do not introduce dashboard styling.

Do not introduce consultant style scorecards.

Do not use decorative UI elements merely to fill space.

Pages 2, 3 and 4 should feel like three members of the same family while still allowing the central mechanic of each idea to determine the composition.

Page 3 should give the original drawing and Studio result substantial visual space.

Page 4 should give the three illustrative brand examples enough visual weight that the mechanic can be understood by scanning the page.

Page 5 should feel deliberately lighter and shorter than the concept pages.

## 5. Copy rules

The copy should read like a smart internal working document written by someone deciding what partnership ideas are worth testing.

Not advertising copy.

Not consulting language.

Not AI generated strategy language.

Not an academic assignment.

Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.

Prefer:

"A parent uploads a child's drawing."

Over:

"Users engage with a family creativity activation."

Prefer:

"A knitted dragon breathes fire."

Over:

"Maker assets are transformed into engaging video outputs."

Prefer:

"Why would the partner want to distribute it?"

Over:

"What is the partner distribution value proposition?"

Do not add cleverness where plain language is stronger.

Do not add new claims merely to make the document sound more impressive.
