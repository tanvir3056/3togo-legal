# Partnership concepts for Creative Fabrica Studio

Tanvir Singh Manku

Three concepts I would test first, who I would contact, and the first 30 days. Every concept brings users to Studio and can be counted. I scored a longer list against five filters; the top three follow in order, the rest sit at the end with their scores.

| Filter | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| User pull | Would a normal consumer actually want to make or share this? |
| Partner incentive | Does the partner have an obvious reason to say yes? |
| Studio fit | Does Studio materially enable it, or could anyone run it? |
| Distribution | Does it bring users beyond CF's existing audience? |

## Concept 1: Turn model launches into consumer campaigns (score 22/25)

Freepik, fal and Krea get the same models on day one and post similar demo content, so availability alone is not a differentiator. What they do not have is CF's users: makers and small sellers who photograph the physical things they make. When a new video model drops, Freepik posts another polished demo clip; a CF user points the same model at a quilt she finished yesterday.

The format, built once and rerun at every launch: **Bring Your Make To Life**. Upload a photo of something you made or sell; the new model animates it. A knitted dragon breathes fire; a seller's product photo becomes a short promo video for her shop. Landing page, entry flow, prompt pack and tracking stay the same; only the model name and example clips change per launch.

**The provider's side:** they compete to be first and noticed, and first is now shared. A wall of real handmade things in motion during launch week is footage no other platform's audience can produce, plus generation volume exactly when they want usage numbers. None of this is a committed deal yet; it is the pitch, and it costs the provider nothing.

**CF's side:** the launch becomes an acquisition event. Entry requires an account and a generation, so every participant is a signup plus an activation, tagged to one landing page. First run measured against the 20,000 users in 14 days bar; that result becomes the baseline the second run must beat. Assets are built per capability, not per date, so a slipped release costs nothing. The possible Kling launch is the first live test; the same kit then runs for every Google, ByteDance and Alibaba drop.

## Concept 2: Kid's Drawing, Made Real (score 21/25)

A child draws something. A parent uploads the drawing and Studio returns it animated, with the child's exact wobbly lines, plus a colouring page, pattern or printable keepsake. The engine is the before-and-after share: original drawing beside the result, child visibly the author, partner brand inside the frame of every share.

Three proof points, all external:

- **IKEA** ran SAGOSKATT for years from 2014: tens of thousands of kids' drawings a year, winners made into real plush sold worldwide, proceeds to children's charities. Families hand brands their children's drawings in volume.
- **Meta** built a free tool that animates kids' drawings; widely used, never productised. Demand proven, product slot empty.
- **Budsies** has sold 76,000+ custom plush from kids' drawings at roughly 99 dollars each. Parents pay for this.

The creativity is visibly the child's, so the concept carries none of the backlash risk of AI generating content from nothing.

Measured per partner via co-branded links: participants, generation rate, shares and prints, return at 14 days. CF can start alone; a brand adds reach.

## Concept 3: Official Creator Challenge (score 20/25)

The repeatable version of a brand activation. The brand supplies an approved asset pack and amplification; CF supplies the generation environment, templates, moderation, landing page and tracking; consumers make and share; the brand reposts the best. Each new brand is a configuration, not a new build, and each challenge has one URL so results read cleanly against the same 14-day bar.

Three windows open right now:

| Vertical | Campaign | Window | Mechanic | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food and beverage | Fanta Haunted Universe | Now to Halloween | 30 second haunted shorts from the official character pack (Fanta owns these characters outright) | Exclude the Ghost Face element, third-party IP |
| Entertainment | Dune Part Three | Opens 18 Dec against Avengers, fans call it Dunesday | Hype films from an official sigil and typography pack | No actor likenesses, ever |
| Gaming | Call of Duty MW4 | Releases 23 Oct, annual franchise | Squad hype reels from a licensed emblem pack | Needs an Activision licence; start that conversation early |

Rules on every challenge, agreed before launch: approved asset packs only, no unlicensed characters or likenesses, human review before anything is featured, brand approval on reposts, written usage terms.

## First 30 days

Four tracks in parallel, not a day-by-day calendar, because model drops do not respect calendars.

1. **Model launch pipeline.** A live provider map: contact route, expected releases, launch opportunity per provider. Pre-build the launch kit (landing page template, prompt packs, tracking, moderation rules) so a drop with hours of notice still ships as a campaign.
2. **Brand pipeline.** Gate rule: no outreach until a target has a concrete activation, a metric and a draft landing page attached. First wave per the table above, sequenced by window.
3. **Reusable campaign kit.** Built once in the first two weeks: page template, per-campaign tracking, moderation with human review (non-negotiable where children's art is involved), standard challenge rules written once with counsel, brand asset intake checklist, one-page post-campaign report ending in a keep or kill call.
4. **Small tests on our own audience.** An owned Halloween craft challenge, a kids' drawing test, a pet portrait pack (Chewy has hand-painted 1,000+ free pet portraits a week for a decade; demand is proven). Not sized to hit the 14-day bar and I will not pretend otherwise; sized to show whether a mechanic produces attributable signups. Keep or kill in writing within a week of each close.

Day 30: provider map live, first outreach out with activations attached, kit used at least once for real, three or more test verdicts on paper.
