The current opening mixes abstraction levels: concept 1 is a partnership category while concepts 2 and 3 are specific activations. Rebuild all three at one level, as activation plays, with these names used consistently on every page:

## Page 1: Three Studio partnership ideas I would test first

Headline: Three Studio partnership ideas I would test first

Subhead: Each gives people a specific reason to create in Studio, gives a partner a reason to distribute the result, and gives Creative Fabrica something measurable in return.

Add one positioning sentence early on this page: Studio sits next to a large maker and design ecosystem, so the raw material for these campaigns comes from things users already create, sell, print, craft or personalise.

The three ideas, one sentence each, as three clean columns or numbered sections:

- 01 Bring Your Make to Life. Launch a new video AI model through things Creative Fabrica users already make.
- 02 Draw It. Bring It to Life. Turn a child's drawing into something that moves, prints, or becomes a keepsake.
- 03 Create With the Brand. Give consumers approved brand assets and a brief, then feature the best creations.

Bottom line: I would test these because the user action is simple, the partner value is visible, and results can be measured quickly.

## Page 2: Bring Your Make to Life

This page is specifically about video AI, not generic model partnerships.

Subhead: When a new video model becomes available in Creative Fabrica Studio, turn the launch into a challenge: upload something you made, animate it in Studio, then share the before and after.

Show the mechanic as three visual steps: 1 Upload (add a photo of something you made or sell), 2 Animate (use the new video model to bring it to life), 3 Share (post the original beside the animated result).

Examples: knitted dragon becomes a short fire-breathing video; hand-drawn illustration starts moving; a seller turns a static product photo into a short promotional clip; crochet character waves, walks or reacts.

Why the video model partner cares: real consumer usage and launch content that looks different from generic AI demos.

Why Creative Fabrica cares: the model release gives existing makers a reason to try Studio and new users a concrete reason to sign up. Participation requires an account and a first generation, attributed through one landing page.

Measure: signups, first video generations, share rate, 14-day return. The first campaign sets the baseline; the second must beat it.

Targets: Kling as the most relevant example if this capability is coming to Studio; Google and ByteDance/BytePlus as a small secondary pipeline note. Phrase Kling as a potential test only.

Keep the scaling note: the kit (landing page template, prompt pack, example format, tracking, moderation rules) is built once and reskinned per release; assets are built around capability, not a calendar date, so a slipped launch costs nothing.

## Page 3: Draw It. Bring It to Life.

Subhead: A child draws the character. Creative Fabrica Studio makes it move or turns it into something the family can keep, print, or make. The original drawing stays visibly at the centre.

Hero visual: one LARGE original-drawing panel, an arrow, one LARGE Studio-result panel. Not two tiny placeholders. If the real images are not supplied with this brief, keep the two frames as large editable image frames and say so in the handback note; never fill them with stock or generated placeholder art.

What Studio can create: a short animation preserving the child's lines; a colouring page; a printable keepsake; a craft or pattern inspired by the drawing.

Why people share it: the drawing already matters to the family. Studio adds motion or a useful format without taking authorship away from the child. This page should feel like a transformation story, not a matrix of corporate precedents.

Partner logic, kept simple:
- HEMA: a practical Dutch pilot; craft, gifting, printing and family occasions already fit the mechanic.
- Crayola: the clearest strategic creativity brand; the idea naturally extends drawing into animation and making.
- IKEA: a strong precedent; IKEA has previously turned children's drawings into physical products. Precedent, not a current programme or partnership.
- Possible later extensions, visually subordinate: Ravensburger, BBC Studios / Bluey, only where rights and approved assets make sense.

Safety line: any child-focused version needs parental consent, careful data handling, moderation, and clear rules for what is stored or shared.

Delete the target-list link and the programme-status footnote line.

## Page 4: Create With the Brand

Subhead: A brand gives Creative Fabrica an approved asset pack and a creative brief. Consumers use those assets inside Studio to make something new. The brand and Creative Fabrica feature the strongest work.

One-line model: approved brand assets + Studio templates + consumer creativity + distribution for the best work.

Examples carry the page, labelled illustrative:
- Fanta at Halloween: short haunted product animations or party graphics from an approved Fanta pack.
- Dune around an entertainment release: fan hype graphics or short videos using only approved symbols, typography or artwork.
- Call of Duty around a game launch: squad intro reels or gaming graphics from an approved franchise pack.

Why a brand cares: consumers spend time creating with the brand instead of only seeing an ad, and the campaign produces a stream of permissioned user work the brand can choose to amplify.

Rights management is part of the product, not a legal footnote: controlled approved assets are why a brand can say yes. Guardrails as one compact band: approved assets only, human review before featuring, partner approval for reposts, written usage terms, no unlicensed likenesses.

## Page 5: Start with a small first wave, not 70 companies

Subhead: The research list is useful for coverage. Outreach stays narrow until each target has a specific idea attached to it.

Three lanes:
- Video AI: Kling first if the Studio opportunity is real and timely; other providers as backup routes, not equal priorities.
- Family creativity: HEMA for a fast local test; Crayola for strategic fit; IKEA as a strong precedent and longer-term route.
- Brand challenges: start with consumer brands where participation is natural and rights are manageable; entertainment and gaming are higher upside but slower because licensing is heavier.

Before contacting any target, answer four questions:
1. What will the user actually make?
2. Why would the partner want to distribute or support it?
3. What does Creative Fabrica gain?
4. What result can we measure quickly?

If one answer is weak, keep researching; do not reach out yet.

If a real workbook URL is supplied, this page carries the document's single link ("Full target research"). Otherwise no link appears anywhere.

## Page 6: First 30 days: build, test, learn, repeat

Subhead: The goal is not a large number of partnership conversations. It is to get enough real evidence to know which ideas deserve more time.

Four blocks:
- Video model launch kit: landing page structure, prompts, example format, tracking and moderation prepared so a new video model can become a campaign quickly.
- First partner wave: contact only targets with a specific consumer idea and a clear reason for the partner to care.
- Reusable campaign basics: standard asset intake, rights checklist, moderation flow, tracking convention, challenge rules, one-page results template.
- Small Creative Fabrica tests: validate mechanics with CF's own audience before waiting for a major partner (Halloween transformation, child drawing test, pet keepsake or pet animation).

Every test ends in a written verdict: keep, change, or stop. Activity without a verdict is not evidence.

At day 30: a short priority partner list with real conversations started; a reusable campaign kit; at least three small test results; a clear view of what to keep, change or stop.
