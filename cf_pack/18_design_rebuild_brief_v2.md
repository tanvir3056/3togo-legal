# Creative Fabrica Studio Partnership Concepts
## Claude Design rebuild brief v2 (final, supersedes every earlier brief)

### What went wrong in the current version

The current version compresses each idea into a campaign slogan and explains the actual idea several lines later, or not at all. "Bring Your Make to Life" forces the reader to decode what "make" means before they know what the idea is. Pages 5 and 6 read like a partnerships operating manual: process vocabulary that buries the creative thinking. The document should make a reader think "this person understands how consumer partnerships could work here", not "this person has produced a structured strategy document".

This brief fixes that. It is a copy and hierarchy rebuild, not a styling pass. The visual system, warmth and restraint of the current version can stay; the words and the page structure change as written below.

### Two rules that govern everything

1. Brutally literal headlines first. Every headline states what the idea actually is, in plain words a first-time reader understands instantly. Campaign-name slogans are never headlines. If a working title genuinely helps, it may appear once, small, inside a concept page (for example: working title, Bring Your Make to Life). When in doubt, leave it out.
2. Every concept page follows the same simple logic, in this order: THE IDEA, WHAT THE USER DOES, WHY THEY WOULD CARE, WHY THE PARTNER CARES, WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES, WHO I WOULD TRY. Nothing else gets its own section. Operational detail (tracking, moderation, kits, checklists) appears only as a single short line where it earns its place, never as a block.

- No claude.ai URLs, browser URLs, timestamps or export artifacts anywhere. Final deliverable is a proper high-resolution PDF export.
- No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons or full stops. Simple arrows are allowed only for actual flows.
- No placeholder links. If a real workbook URL is supplied with this brief, ONE modest link on page 5, labelled "Full target research". Otherwise no link anywhere.
- Text-layer integrity: every visible space is a real space character. Verify by copy-pasting the page 1 headline and one paragraph from each page out of the exported PDF.
- No Creative Fabrica logo, no third-party logos, no score bubbles, no filter pills.
- No invented commitments, dates, prizes or partnerships. Kling is a potential test, never announced. IKEA's kids-drawing programme is precedent, never current. Brand examples are illustrative.
- Keep the footer: page number plus "Working document, not official Creative Fabrica material". Keep the byline Tanvir Singh Manku and the top-right working-document label on page 1.

# Page-by-page rebuild copy

## Page 1

Headline: Three partnership ideas I would test for Creative Fabrica Studio

No positioning paragraph. Go straight to the three ideas as three numbered blocks. Each block: literal title, then one or two plain sentences.

1. **Turn what you made into a video.** Use new AI video models to animate the crafts, illustrations and products people already make and sell.

2. **Turn your kid's drawing into something real.** A drawing becomes an animation, a printable, a craft or a keepsake. Built for family and creativity partnerships.

3. **Let fans create with official brand assets.** Give consumers approved assets from brands they love and let them make something worth sharing.

That is the whole page. It alone must tell the reader exactly what the three ideas are.

## Page 2: Turn what you made into a video

THE IDEA
When a new video model launches in Studio, run a two week challenge around it. Upload a photo of something you made, the model animates it, share the original beside the result.

WHAT THE USER DOES
Uploads a photo of a crochet toy, an illustration, a craft or a product they sell. Studio animates it with the newly launched model. They post the before and after.

WHY THEY WOULD CARE
A knitted dragon breathes fire. A hand-drawn character starts moving. A seller's static product photo becomes a short promotional clip for her shop. The thing they already love gets a second life.

WHY THE PARTNER CARES
Every platform gets the same model on day one and posts the same polished demo clips. Creative Fabrica's users are the only audience that can feed a video model real handmade things. That is launch content nobody else can produce, at exactly the moment the model maker wants usage.

WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES
Entering needs an account and a first generation, so every participant is a signup plus an activation, tracked through one landing page. The challenge kit is built once and rerun at every model launch.

WHO I WOULD TRY
Kling first, if this capability is coming to Studio; a potential test only, nothing announced. Google and ByteDance as the next routes when their models land.

Keep the three-step Upload, Animate, Share visual if it fits; it supports WHAT THE USER DOES.

## Page 3: Turn your kid's drawing into something real

THE IDEA
A parent uploads a child's drawing. Studio animates it keeping the child's exact lines, or turns it into a colouring page, a printable keepsake, or a craft the family can make.

Hero visual stays: one LARGE original-drawing panel, an arrow, one LARGE Studio-result panel. If the real images are not supplied, keep the frames as large editable image frames and say so in the handback note; never fill them with stock or generated placeholder art.

WHAT THE USER DOES
Takes a photo of the drawing, uploads it, picks what it becomes, shares the drawing next to the result.

WHY THEY WOULD CARE
The drawing already matters to the family. The child stays visibly the author; Studio adds motion or a keepsake without replacing their work. "My five year old designed this" is the most shared kind of parent content there is.

WHY THE PARTNER CARES
Crayola extends from art materials into AI-assisted creativity. HEMA gets a fast, practical Dutch family campaign that fits its craft and printing aisles. IKEA has already turned children's drawings into real products; precedent, not a current programme.

WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES
Family signups from far outside the current audience, a naturally shareable before and after, and an AI story with no backlash risk because the creativity is visibly the child's.

WHO I WOULD TRY
HEMA for a fast local pilot, Crayola for the strategic version, IKEA as the longer route. One safety line, small: any child-focused version needs parental consent, careful data handling and human moderation.

## Page 4: Let fans create with official brand assets

THE IDEA
A brand provides an approved pack of characters, artwork or typography. Creative Fabrica turns it into Studio templates. Fans make their own graphics or short videos with it. The brand features the best ones.

WHAT THE USER DOES
Picks a template, creates with real official assets from a brand they love, enters the challenge, shares the result.

WHY THEY WOULD CARE
Making something with the real assets, not fan-art knockoffs, plus a chance to be featured by the brand itself.

Examples, labelled illustrative, carry the page:
- Fanta at Halloween: short haunted animations or party graphics from an approved Fanta pack.
- Dune around the next film: fan hype videos using only approved sigils and typography, never actor likenesses.
- Call of Duty at launch: squad intro reels from a licensed emblem pack.

WHY THE PARTNER CARES
Fans spend time creating with the brand instead of only seeing an ad, and the campaign produces a stream of permissioned fan work the brand can choose to amplify. Approved asset packs are the reason a brand's legal team can say yes.

WHY CREATIVE FABRICA CARES
Every contestant is a signed-up user, and each new brand is a configuration of the same challenge kit, not a new build.

WHO I WOULD TRY
Fanta while the Halloween window is open, Warner Bros around the Dune release, Activision early because game licensing takes longest.

## Page 5: Where I would start

Short page. It closes the document; it is not an operating manual.

Opening line: Behind these three ideas sits a research list of 70 companies. I would not contact 70 companies. I would start narrow.

Three lanes, one or two lines each:
- Video AI: Kling first if the Studio opportunity is real and timely, other providers as backup routes.
- Family creativity: HEMA for speed, Crayola for scale, IKEA for the long game.
- Brand challenges: consumer brands first because rights are manageable; entertainment and gaming later because licensing is slower.

Before contacting anyone, four questions:
1. What will the user actually make?
2. Why would the partner want to distribute it?
3. What does Creative Fabrica gain?
4. What result can we measure quickly?

If one answer is weak, keep researching, do not reach out.

Closing line: Each mechanic can be tested first on Creative Fabrica's own audience, so evidence starts arriving before any partner signs anything.
