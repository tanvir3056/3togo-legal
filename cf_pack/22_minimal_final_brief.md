# Partnership ideas for Creative Fabrica Studio
## Final brief: minimal rebuild. This supersedes every earlier brief.

## The goal

Five quiet pages that read like a sharp person set down their ideas the night before an interview. Minimal, almost plain. The thinking is the design. If a page would look at home in a corporate template library, it is wrong; if it would look at home as a beautifully typeset letter, it is right.

## Design rules

1. White background. Dark, near-black text. At most one muted accent colour, used sparingly (page numbers, the thin rule under the headline). No background tints, no coloured panels.
2. One typeface family. Three sizes only: headline, body, small (footers and captions). Nothing bold inside body text except at most one phrase per page.
3. Single column of text with wide margins. Text starts at the left margin and flows. No cards, no boxes, no grids, no columns, no sidebars.
4. No icons, no pills, no chips, no tags, no dividers between sections, no decorative anything. A single thin rule under the page headline is the only ornament allowed.
5. The two images on page 3 are the only visual in the document.
6. Number the ideas 1, 2, 3, 4. Not 01. Headlines in sentence case, as a person types.
7. Footers: one small, quiet, grey, sentence-case line. No capital-letter warning strips. No name in any footer; the byline at the top of page 1 is the only place the name appears.
8. Vary the rhythm: paragraphs of different lengths, including single-sentence paragraphs. Pages do not need to mirror each other. Perfect repetition between pages is what makes documents look generated.
9. Each page carries at most 130 words of body text. Do not add anything to fill space. Empty space stays empty.

### Page 1

Headline: Four partnership ideas for Creative Fabrica Studio

Then the four ideas, each as a numbered title plus one plain sentence:

1. Turn your creations into a promo video. When a new video model lands in Studio, run a challenge: upload a photo of something you made or sell, and the model brings it to life.

2. Make your kid's drawing come alive. A parent uploads the drawing. Studio turns it into an animation, a colouring page, a craft or a keepsake.

3. Challenge fans to create with a brand's official assets. The brand provides an approved asset pack, fans make things with it, the brand features the best.

4. Turn one product shoot into weeks of ads and content. Studio sits between the assets a brand already owns and the volume of creative it has to publish.

### Page 2

Headline: Turn your creations into a promo video

Every AI platform gets the same models on day one, and they all post the same demo clips. What they do not have is an audience with real handmade things to point a model at.

So when a new video model lands in Studio, make the launch a challenge: upload a photo of something you made or sell, animate it, share the before and after. A knitted dragon breathes fire. A hand-drawn character starts moving. A seller's product photo becomes a short promo clip for her shop.

The model maker gets launch content that looks nothing like another demo. Creative Fabrica gets a signup and a first generation from every entry.

I would prove it on Creative Fabrica's own audience first, then bring the results to a provider like Kling.

Footer: Kling and other providers are illustrative, nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 2 / 5

### Page 3

Headline: Make your kid's drawing come alive

A child draws something. A parent uploads it. Studio makes it move, keeping the wobbly lines exactly as drawn, or turns it into a colouring page, a craft or a printable keepsake.

Then the two large image frames side by side (original drawing, Studio result), with tiny captions. They stay empty editable frames until real images are supplied; never fill them with stock or generated art.

People already love this. IKEA turned kids' drawings into real plush toys for years. Meta built a tool that animates children's drawings and it went viral. Budsies has sold tens of thousands of plushies made from kids' drawings. The child stays the author, which is why it feels like magic instead of AI replacing creativity.

I would try HEMA for a fast Dutch pilot, Crayola for the big version, IKEA as the long game.

Small: Any child-focused version needs parental consent, careful data handling and human review.

Footer: Brands mentioned are illustrative, nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 3 / 5

### Page 4

Headline: Challenge fans to create with a brand's official assets

A brand hands over an approved asset pack: characters, artwork, typography. Creative Fabrica turns it into Studio templates and a landing page. Fans make their own graphics and short videos with real official assets, and the brand features the best work.

Think Fanta at Halloween: haunted clips and party graphics from the official character pack. A film release: fan hype videos from approved artwork, never actors' faces. A game launch: squad intro reels from licensed emblems.

Approved assets are the reason a brand's legal team can say yes. Every contestant is a signed-up user, and the second brand costs a fraction of the first.

I would start with an owned Creative Fabrica challenge, then take the evidence to seasonal consumer brands.

Footer: All brands are illustrative, nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 4 / 5

### Page 5

Headline: Turn one product shoot into weeks of ads and content

An online shop pays once for product photos, a logo, brand guidelines. Then it has to publish forever: paid ads, organic posts, short video, seasonal variations. Studio can sit in that gap: one approved asset set in, weeks of usable creative out.

The timing is good. Ad delivery is increasingly automated (Meta's Andromeda is part of that shift), and automated delivery rewards whoever can feed it more variations to test.

One agency relationship puts Studio into workflows across many brands at once, which beats winning shops one at a time. I would test with a single performance agency, one product, one batch of outputs, and let them judge whether they would run it again.

Footer: Agencies mentioned are illustrative, nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 5 / 5
