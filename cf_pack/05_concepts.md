# 05: Three partnership concepts

Built to the CEO's units (users, generations, posts, days) and the bare-bones constraint: runnable by one person plus the new content team, existing product surfaces, no engineering beyond a landing page and templates. All brand work runs on the moderated tier of the API/Studio, never the NSFW-capable tier.

---

## Concept 1 (inbound, the Monday email): The Kling Co-Launch Playbook

### 1. Name and pitch
"Kling day one, with a challenge." Turn Kling's next model drop from an API listing into a tracked acquisition event: day-one availability in Studio wrapped in a 14-day maker challenge aimed at 20,000 tracked sign-ups.

### 2. Why now
- Per the CEO on the 27 Aug call, CF is talking to Kling and a new model may land within weeks. Public evidence (cadence, September precedent, rivals passing Kling's 15s cap) supports a September 3.5-class video refresh, MEDIUM confidence (see 02).
- CF already carries Kling 3.0 and V3 Omni, so the integration risk is near zero; the missing piece is the marketing wrapper and the formal launch-partner slot.

### 3. The mechanic
- Kling grants CF API access to the new model 1 to 2 weeks pre-launch under embargo, and names CF as launch partner in its announcement (the pattern fal, Picsart and Replicate already enjoy; see 02).
- CF builds a "Kling Craft Challenge" template pack tuned to the model's headline capability, with three pre-built variants so launch day only needs a headline swap: (a) longer duration: "your make-along in one clip", a craft project start to finish using multi-shot storyboard; (b) audio: "narrated maker story" with voice-over prompts; (c) image/motion refresh: "bring your design to life", animating a marketplace design or product photo.
- Day one: model goes live in Studio, landing page opens, CF and Kling co-post within the same hour; CF channel team (TikTok, Reddit, Instagram, X) pushes the challenge; email to the registered base.
- Users generate their entry in Studio from a challenge template, then post it to TikTok/Instagram/X with the campaign hashtag and to a CF gallery page.
- Days 2 to 14: daily featured entries on CF channels, Kling reposts the best (their community reach plus affiliate network), winners get 12 months of Studio plus Kling credits funded by Kling.
- Every challenge asset links to one landing page; entries require a CF account, so participation is a tracked sign-up plus at least one generation (activation built in).

### 4. Give and get
| Kling gives | CF gives |
|---|---|
| Pre-launch model access under embargo | Day-one distribution to millions of registered users in a vertical Kling does not reach (crafters, POD sellers) |
| Named launch-partner slot in the announcement | Co-branded launch post, tutorial content, SEO landing page for the model |
| Launch credits and prize funding for the challenge | A wall of themed UGC in launch week (the adoption story Kling shows next to its release) |
| Reposts via Kling channels and affiliate network | Committed inference volume on the new model |

### 5. Assets and effort (day-one checklist)
- Landing page with challenge brief and gallery: marketing engineer plus designer, ~16 hours.
- Template pack (10 to 15 prompts/templates per capability variant): content team, ~24 hours, reusable.
- Launch post kit (co-post copy, channel assets, email): content team plus PMM, ~12 hours.
- In-product entry point (banner/tile in Studio linking the challenge): existing surface, config not engineering.
- Tracking setup (UTMs, campaign tag, hashtag monitor): ~4 hours.
- Total: roughly one week of the content team's time; repeatable for every future model launch (the playbook is the real asset).

### 6. Tracking and KPI
- Attribution: dedicated landing page, UTM on every Kling-side and CF-side link, campaign tag on the in-product entry point, "where did you hear about this" at sign-up as a check. Partner-sourced = signed up via campaign page or tag.
- Target: 20,000 new users in 14 days (the CEO's feature-scale unit) attributed to the launch, of whom 50%+ make a first generation in week one.
- Secondary: generations on the new model, hashtag post count, gallery entries, D7 retention of the cohort vs paid cohorts, subscriber conversion at day 30.
- Failure line: under 5,000 tracked sign-ups in 14 days, or activation below 25%: then the mechanic (not the channel) gets redesigned before the next launch.

### 7. Risks and guardrails
- Provider timing slips last minute (the CEO expects this): all assets are date-portable and capability-keyed; slip costs nothing but the wait.
- Embargo discipline: no public tease until Kling posts; one named owner each side.
- Wrong capability guess: covered by the three pre-built variants.
- Moderation: challenge runs on the moderated tier; gallery entries reviewed before featuring.
- Dependency: if Kling declines the formal slot, run the challenge anyway on day-of-API-wave access; the playbook still ships and the numbers still count.

### 8. First three targets
1. Kling API/BD team via klingai_api_platform@kuaishou.com and the business-inquiry form on klingai.com/global/dev: the published commercial route; reference CF's live V3 Omni integration. Opening line: "We already run Kling 3.0 and V3 Omni in production for a crafter audience of millions; for your next release we want to be in the announcement, not just the API wave."
2. Tony Pu, Community & Partnerships at Kling AI (public LinkedIn headline, verify first): the community/co-marketing half (reposts, affiliate push, prize credits).
3. Kuaishou overseas BD via the Creative Partner Program and partnership page (kling.ai/app/future-partner/creator): the fallback route if 1 and 2 stall.

### 9. Partner pitch (to Kling)
We run your models in production for one of the largest crafting and design communities in the world: millions of registered users who make and post physical things. For your next launch we want a named launch-partner slot: give us access two weeks early under embargo and repost our community's best work. On day one your model goes live in our Studio wrapped in a 14-day maker challenge, with our TikTok, Reddit and Instagram channels and our email base behind it. You get a vertical adoption story no other aggregator gives you: real people making real crafts with your model in launch week, in a wall of tagged UGC. We commit inference volume and full embargo discipline.

### 10. Three-line version (for the Monday email)
Kling's next drop should be a campaign, not a listing: day one in Studio, wrapped in a 14-day maker challenge with a template pack tuned to the model's headline feature, co-posted by Kling. Everything runs through one tracked landing page, aiming at your 20,000-users-in-14-days number, and the same playbook then reruns for every Google and ByteDance launch. The assets are capability-keyed, so if Kling slips the date nothing is wasted.

## Concept 2 (outbound): "Make the Gift" brand activation, Q4 2026

### 1. Name and pitch
"Make the Gift" with Crayola (or Cricut/Funko). A Q4 personalised-gifting challenge where a brand's licensed assets live inside Studio, users design and physically make gifts, and both sides get the headline: the Netflix-precedent structure with tracking added.

### 2. Why now
- Q4 is the craft season: hobbyist gift-makers are active Oct to early Dec, and sellers stock even earlier; personalisation is the top-selling attribute on seasonal listings (MEDIUM).
- Brands need an AI story that is not "AI slop": the 2025 Coca-Cola and McDonald's NL backlashes punished brand-made AI ads. User-made, AI-assisted, physically finished craft is the defensible framing, and CF is the only platform whose output is a real object.

### 3. The mechanic
- Brand supplies a walled, licensed asset pack (palettes, characters, packaging elements) available only inside a branded Studio flow: constrained co-creation keeps output brand-safe (the Burger King Million Dollar Whopper model).
- User designs a personalised gift (card, ornament, colouring page, wrapped jar, display case), makes it physically, posts photo/video with the campaign hashtag; entry lives in a moderated CF gallery with brand right-of-review before any featuring.
- Brand amplifies on its channels and funds prizes; CF supplies the tools, the audience, and daily featured makes; both sides get the press line ("[Brand] puts its icons in the hands of a million makers").
- Primary candidates: Crayola (SVP Global Licensing named publicly; already ran a 2025 global open call for user art, so the buying behaviour is proven), Cricut (near-total audience overlap, machine sales peak in Q4, licensed-IP precedent in Design Space), Funko (new CEO built Netflix's brand-partnership machine; Pop!-style personalisation is native to crafting).
- Pantone backup with a built-in date: Color of the Year lands early Dec; a CotY 2027 craft asset pack is a low-lift, high-headline variant.

### 4. Give and get
| Brand gives | CF gives |
|---|---|
| Licensed asset pack and right to use its name | The branded Studio flow, templates, gallery, moderation |
| Amplification on its social/email/PR channels | A maker audience that produces content around the product instead of viewing an ad |
| Prize funding (product plus subscriptions) | Daily featuring, tracked UGC wall, campaign report |
| The headline for CF | The headline for the brand (real hands, real crafts, AI-assisted) |

### 5. Assets and effort
- Branded landing page and gallery: ~16 hours. Asset pack ingestion and 10 to 12 templates: content team, ~24 hours. Legal: licence terms for the pack (personal use only, no POD resale unless brand allows: critical for CF's seller audience). Moderation rota during the campaign. No engineering beyond existing surfaces.

### 6. Tracking and KPI
- Same rails as Concept 1: landing page, UTMs, in-product tag, hashtag.
- Target: 20,000 tracked sign-ups in the first 14 days of the campaign window; secondary: UGC count (posts plus gallery entries), brand-side reach, activation and D7 of the cohort.
- Failure line: under 5,000 sign-ups or a UGC count so low the brand cannot headline it (under ~500 public makes).

### 7. Risks and guardrails
- IP: everything runs from the licensed pack; no generation of brand marks outside it; brand right of review before amplification.
- Brand safety: moderated tier only, human review of gallery, clear no-NSFW and no-hate terms.
- Seller confusion: state clearly whether outputs may be sold (default: personal use only).
- Timeline: brand deals take 6 to 10 weeks; to hit Q4 the first conversations must start in September, which is exactly the week-one pipeline argument.

### 8. First three targets
1. Kim Rompilla, SVP Global Licensing, Crayola (publicly named, License Global): "Creative Acts of Color x Creative Fabrica", the adult-crafter extension of their open-call platform.
2. Cricut partnerships/content (via investor-relations press contact; no name public): "design in Studio, cut on Cricut" Q4 make-along.
3. Josh Simon, CEO, Funko (publicly named; ex-Netflix consumer products chief): personalised display and party-kit crafting around Pop! collecting.

### 9. Partner pitch (to Crayola)
Your Creative Acts of Color campaign asked the world to make art. We can hand it a million adult makers. Creative Fabrica is the platform where crafters design and physically make things: cards, ornaments, colouring pages, gifts. For Q4 we would build a walled Crayola flow in our Studio: your palettes and icons, our AI tools, and a "Make the Gift" challenge where every entry is a real object someone made by hand. You get user-made creativity at scale (the safe side of the AI story), a tracked UGC wall, and the press line. We get your amplification. Both sides get the headline.

### 10. Three-line version
A Q4 "Make the Gift" campaign with a craft-adjacent brand (Crayola, Cricut or Funko): their licensed assets in a walled Studio flow, users design and physically make gifts, post with the hashtag, brand amplifies and funds prizes. It is the Netflix-style attention play with tracking added: one landing page, a 20,000-user 14-day target, and a UGC wall both sides can headline.

## Concept 3 (distribution): Studio as the SMB seller's creative engine

### 1. Name and pitch
"Where sellers already work." Put Studio in front of Etsy, POD and Shopify sellers through the platforms they already use, riding the structural demand for creative volume that ad platforms now enforce. A channel measured in users, not a sales quota.

### 2. Why now
- All three ad platforms moved targeting into black-box AI and made creative volume the advertiser's main lever: Meta's Andromeda ranking rewards genuinely diverse creatives (a controlled 25-creative test: +17% conversions at -16% cost, MEDIUM); TikTok recommends 5 to 7 assets per ad group refreshed weekly (MEDIUM); Google PMax wants up to 40+ assets per asset group (HIGH). SMBs cannot meet this by hand.
- Nobody owns the POD/marketplace-seller niche end to end: the ad-creative specialists (AdCreative, Creatify) chase generic e-commerce, the suites (Freepik, Canva) chase teams. CF's audience already is the niche.

### 3. The mechanic
- Shopify App Store listing: a CF Studio app that turns a product URL into listing images and ad-creative sets ($19 listing fee, 0% revenue share to the first $1M; AdCreative and Creatify prove the category). The one item needing real engineering: flag it and sequence it after the zero-engineering moves below.
- POD design-layer integrations: CF exports print-ready files into Printful/Printify (post-merger one deal may cover both) and Gelato, with co-marketed "holiday shop launch" challenges for sellers. Uses the existing B2B API as the integration surface; the separate Founding AE owns any commercial API contract, this concept only opens the door.
- Etsy lane: seller-enablement content partnership (holiday design-trend drops plus tools), and recruitment of craft bloggers/YouTubers into CF's existing affiliate program (25%/20% commissions) via Awin, where Etsy's own program lives.
- Ad-platform credibility layer, later: Meta Business Partners (Creative specialty) and TikTok Marketing Partners badges once an export-to-ads flow exists.
- Every route lands on segment-specific landing pages ("Studio for Etsy sellers", "Studio for POD") with tracked funnels.

### 4. Give and get
| Partner gives | CF gives |
|---|---|
| Distribution at the moment of need (app store search, seller dashboards, affiliate content) | Better-stocked sellers and listings (their GMV) |
| Co-marketing to seller bases | A design layer they do not have to build |
| Affiliate reach in the craft niche | 25%/20% recurring commissions (program already exists) |

### 5. Assets and effort
- Zero-engineering first wave: affiliate recruitment via Awin/Impact, seller landing pages, co-marketed seller challenges with Printful/Printify/Gelato: content team plus one BD thread, ~2 weeks.
- Engineering wave (flagged, needs product buy-in): Shopify app, print-ready export polish. Downgraded to quarter two of the plan for that reason.

### 6. Tracking and KPI
- Per-partner UTM funnels and affiliate links; partner-sourced sign-ups per route per month.
- Target: this is baseline-lift, not spike: 10,000 partner-sourced sign-ups per month by month three across routes, with cohort quality (activation, D30, subscriber conversion) reported against paid cohorts. A launch-scale moment (Shopify app going live) gets the 20,000-in-14-days treatment.
- Failure line: any route under 1,000 sign-ups/month or with activation under half of paid cohorts after 60 days gets cut.

### 7. Risks and guardrails
- Not enterprise, by design: Studio for consumers/SMBs; the API's commercial deals belong to the Founding AE seat. This concept opens platform doors, it does not sell contracts.
- Frenemy surfaces: Canva Apps would distribute CF inside a competitor; decide policy before building (default: no).
- Etsy sensitivity: CF sellers sell there; frame as seller enablement, never competition.
- AI-content disclosure rules on marketplaces (Etsy requires disclosure): bake into templates and guidance.

### 8. First three targets
1. Printful/Printify partnerships (Head of Partnerships/Ecosystem, no name public; partner pages): post-merger they need seller-acquisition stories; one conversation may cover both brands.
2. Awin advertiser team: extend CF's existing affiliate program with a targeted craft-creator recruitment push (Etsy's program lives there, so the publisher base is exactly right).
3. Gelato partnerships (Oslo: EMEA-friendly): "local production, global creativity" EU holiday co-marketing.

### 9. Partner pitch (to Printful/Printify)
Your growth is new sellers who upload designs that sell. Our platform is where those designs get made: a marketplace of 15M+ licensed assets and an AI Studio built for crafters and POD sellers. We want to be your design layer: print-ready files straight from Studio into your catalogue, plus a co-marketed holiday shop-launch challenge that pushes our millions of registered makers to open their first store with you. You get sellers and catalogue-ready designs; we get your seller base meeting Studio at the moment they need designs. Tracked links both ways, so we both see the number.

### 10. Three-line version
Ad platforms now force SMBs to produce 5 to 40 creative variants per campaign, and nobody serves the seller niche CF already owns. Distribution partnerships (Printful/Printify and Gelato integrations, craft-creator affiliates via Awin, later a Shopify app) put Studio where Etsy and POD sellers already work. Measured as a channel: partner-sourced sign-ups per route per month, cohort quality against paid, losers cut at 60 days.
