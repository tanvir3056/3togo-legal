# Creative Fabrica Studio Partnership Ideas
## Simplification brief: ideas only

The document currently reads like an AI-generated strategy deck. The reason is the template: every page carries the same set of capital-letter labelled blocks (THE IDEA, THE INSIGHT, WHY THIS PARTNERSHIP WORKS, WHO I WOULD TEST IT WITH FIRST, FIRST TEST, metric grids), all perfectly parallel. Real ideas documents do not look like that. This pass strips the document down to the ideas themselves.

## Structural changes

1. DELETE page 6 entirely. The document becomes five pages. Renumber footers to N / 5.
2. On pages 2 to 5, DELETE these elements completely: the THE INSIGHT sidebar, the WHY THIS PARTNERSHIP WORKS header with its FOR X / FOR Y columns, the WHO I WOULD TEST IT WITH FIRST block, the FIRST TEST block, every metric grid, the RIGHTS GUARDRAILS grid, and the FOCUS / PARTNER label rows. The content worth keeping from them is already folded into the body copy below.
3. Remove almost all capital-letter section labels. Each concept page is: a headline, body text, examples, one closing line. Prose, not labelled boxes.
4. Pages may look different from each other. Let each idea's content drive its layout. Symmetry between pages is not a goal; it is part of the problem.
5. Generous whitespace. Each concept page carries at most 130 words of body text. Nothing else may be added to fill the space.

### Page 2

Headline: Turn your creations into a promo video

Body:

Every AI platform gets the same models on day one, and they all post the same demo clips. What they do not have is an audience with real handmade things to point a model at.

So when a new video model lands in Studio, make the launch a challenge: upload a photo of something you made or sell, animate it, share the before and after. A knitted dragon breathes fire. A hand-drawn character starts moving. A seller's product photo becomes a short promo clip for her shop.

The model maker gets launch content that looks nothing like another demo. Creative Fabrica gets a signup and a first generation from every entry.

I would prove it on Creative Fabrica's own audience first, then bring the results to a provider like Kling.

Footer: Kling and other providers are illustrative. Nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 2 / 5

### Page 3

Headline: Make your kid's drawing come alive

Keep the two large image frames (original drawing, Studio result) as the centrepiece. They stay empty editable frames until real images are supplied; never stock or generated art.

Body:

A child draws something. A parent uploads it. Studio makes it move, keeping the wobbly lines exactly as drawn, or turns it into a colouring page, a craft or a printable keepsake.

People already love this. IKEA turned kids' drawings into real plush toys for years. Meta built a tool that animates children's drawings and it went viral. Budsies has sold tens of thousands of plushies made from kids' drawings. The child stays the author, which is why it feels like magic instead of AI replacing creativity.

I would try HEMA for a fast Dutch pilot, Crayola for the big version, IKEA as the long game.

Small line at the bottom of the body: Any child-focused version needs parental consent, careful data handling and human review.

Footer: Brands named are illustrative. Nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 3 / 5

### Page 4

Headline: Challenge fans to create with a brand's official assets

Body:

A brand hands over an approved asset pack: characters, artwork, typography. Creative Fabrica turns it into Studio templates and a landing page. Fans make their own graphics and short videos with real official assets, and the brand features the best work.

Think Fanta at Halloween: haunted clips and party graphics from the official character pack. A film release: fan hype videos from approved artwork, never actors' faces. A game launch: squad intro reels from licensed emblems.

Approved assets are the reason a brand's legal team can say yes. Every contestant is a signed-up user, and the second brand costs a fraction of the first.

I would start with an owned Creative Fabrica challenge, then take the evidence to seasonal consumer brands.

Footer: All brands illustrative. Nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 4 / 5

### Page 5

Headline: Turn one product shoot into weeks of ads and content

Body:

An online shop pays once for product photos, a logo, brand guidelines. Then it has to publish forever: paid ads, organic posts, short video, seasonal variations. Studio can sit in that gap: one approved asset set in, weeks of usable creative out.

The timing is good. Ad delivery is increasingly automated (Meta's Andromeda is part of that shift), and automated delivery rewards whoever can feed it more variations to test.

One agency relationship puts Studio into workflows across many brands at once, which beats winning shops one at a time. I would test with a single performance agency, one product, one batch of outputs, and let them judge whether they would run it again.

Footer: Agencies named are illustrative. Nothing is implied or agreed. Working document. 5 / 5

## Design rules for this pass

- No capital-letter section labels inside pages. No pills, no cards laid out in grids, no KPI tiles.
- Typography does the work: headline, body, one size for emphasis. As if a sharp person laid out their own ideas in a nice document.
- The page 3 image pair is the only mandatory visual. Elsewhere a single simple visual per page is allowed only if it explains the idea better than the words; otherwise none.
- Keep the warm editorial feel, colours and fonts. This is a copy and layout simplification, not a rebrand.
- Still binding: no em or en dashes anywhere, no URLs, no logos, no score bubbles, nothing presented as agreed or in progress.
- Rename the document so the exported PDF's title is "Partnership ideas for Creative Fabrica Studio".
