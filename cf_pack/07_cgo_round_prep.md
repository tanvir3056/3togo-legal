## Growth-language cheat sheet

One line each. Use these words naturally; do not recite.

- CAC: cost to acquire one user or subscriber; total spend divided by users acquired.
- Blended vs paid CAC: blended spreads spend over all sign-ups (organic included); paid divides ad spend by paid-attributed sign-ups only. Partnerships lower blended CAC.
- Activation: the first moment of value; at CF, plausibly the first generation in Studio, not the sign-up.
- First generation: the first asset a new user creates; the activation event partnerships should be judged on.
- D7 / D30 retention: share of a sign-up cohort still active 7 or 30 days later; tells you if partner-sourced users are real or tourists.
- LTV: revenue a user generates over their lifetime; subscriber conversion times average subscription length.
- Payback: months until a user's revenue covers their acquisition cost; partnerships aim for near-zero cost, so near-instant payback.
- Attribution: tying a sign-up to its source: UTM-tagged links, dedicated landing pages, promo codes, in-product "how did you hear" tags.
- Partner-sourced vs partner-influenced: sourced means the sign-up came through the partner's link or code; influenced means the campaign touched them but another channel closed. Report both, promise only sourced.
- Incrementality: users you would not have got anyway; the honest test of a channel.
- Holdout: a group deliberately not exposed to the campaign, to measure incrementality.
- Creative fatigue: ad performance decays as an audience sees the same creative; drives demand for creative volume.
- UGC as distribution: every user post is free reach; the campaign's output is also its media plan.
- Earned media value: what the coverage and posts would have cost as paid media; soft number, use as secondary.
- Launch spike vs baseline lift: the day-one bump vs the permanently higher line after; a good partnership moves both.
- Cohort quality: partner-sourced cohorts compared with paid cohorts on activation, retention, conversion; the metric that decides if the channel scales.

## 15 likely questions, with model answers

Answers use only claimable facts. Keep each answer under 45 seconds live.

1. "How would you attribute sign-ups to a partner launch?"
   Dedicated landing page per partnership, UTM on every partner-side link, a campaign tag on the in-product entry point, and a "where did you hear about this" option at sign-up as a check. Report partner-sourced as the number, partner-influenced as context. Then track that cohort to first generation and D7, because the CGO cares whether they stay.

2. "What is a good partnership versus a fun one?"
   A good one has a number before it starts: sign-ups in the CEO's units (20k in 14 days for a feature, 100k in 7 for a product, per Roemie on our call), a tracked route, and a cohort that activates. A fun one has a headline and no landing page. Netflix-style attention plays are fine, but I would add tracking to them; attention and measurement are not enemies.

3. "How many partner conversations a week would you run?"
   Pipeline like a machine: 20 to 30 new outreach touches a week, 8 to 10 live conversations, 2 to 3 in negotiation, aiming to launch something every 3 to 4 weeks once the engine runs. At Voicing I kept a seven-firm partner channel warm simultaneously (EY, Deloitte, LTIMindtree, Capgemini, Coforge, Teleperformance, Wipro); cadence and follow-up discipline is the transferable part.

4. "What do you cut after 30 days?"
   Anything where the partner cannot commit a date, a channel, or an audience. And any live campaign whose cohort does not activate: if partner-sourced users generate nothing in week one, the mechanic is wrong, fix or kill. I would pre-agree a failure number per campaign so cutting is automatic, not a debate.

5. "Tell me about a partnership that failed and what you learned."
   A travel-technology POC I co-funded with a global SI partner (about EUR 80K) taught me the cost of unclear ownership: two partners, each assuming the other drove adoption. Since then every deal I run has one named owner per side and a written next-step cadence. I would carry that into CF: every brand deal gets a one-page run sheet.

6. "You have never done consumer co-marketing. Why will you be good at it?"
   True, my paid experience is small scale: I run my band's marketing myself, Meta ads, creatives, using AI tools for creative volume. What I have done for four-plus years is make partners commit resources to a shared outcome and then run the delivery. The consumer mechanics are learnable and I have already drafted three (shows the concepts); the persistence and pipeline discipline are the hard part, and that is proven.

7. "Isn't this role just sales with another name?"
   See the one-paragraph answer below.

8. "How do you prioritise inbound (model providers) against outbound (brands)?"
   Inbound first in weeks 1 to 4: the providers are already at the door, the leverage is unusual (per Roemie, Google texts CF pre-launch), and each launch is a free acquisition spike if we wrap a mechanic round it. Outbound brand deals have 6 to 10 week lead times, so I start those conversations in week 1 too, aimed at Q4.

9. "What would you do in your first 30 days?"
   Week 1: map every live provider relationship, who holds it, what was promised. Weeks 1 to 2: ship a co-launch playbook so the next model drop (Kling is imminent, per Roemie) runs as a campaign, not just a release. Weeks 2 to 4: open 15 to 20 brand and distribution conversations from a prioritised list I already have (03_hitlist.csv). Report weekly in users, not meetings.

10. "How would you measure a Netflix-style attention play where conversion is not the goal?"
    Primary: tracked sign-ups from the campaign page anyway, because attention that cannot be found later is a rumour. Secondary: UGC count with the campaign tag, reach of partner posts, press pickups, and the cohort's activation rate. I would still set a floor number; "attention" with zero tracked users is a failure.

11. "The partner slips the launch date last minute. What do you do?"
    Expect it; Roemie said Kling might move next week's launch last minute. Everything I build is date-portable: templates, landing page, and challenge copy keyed to capability, not date. Slip means we hold assets, keep the wave, and use the wait to pre-seed creators under embargo where allowed.

12. "How do you work with a Product Marketing Manager without stepping on them?"
    PMM owns the launch narrative and targets; I own the partner's contribution to it: their channels, credits, co-posts, and the joint mechanic. One shared launch doc, PMM holds the pen. At Voicing I ran exactly this seam between partner marketing and our own launches with SI partners.

13. "What does the partner actually get from tiny Creative Fabrica?"
    Distribution to millions of registered users who make and post things, day-one availability that makes the provider's launch look adopted, and a wall of UGC in a niche (crafts, personalisation) the other aggregators do not own. For brands: a maker audience that produces content around the product instead of just viewing an ad.

14. "How do you avoid becoming a bottleneck as one person?"
    Playbooks over heroics: one repeatable co-launch template for providers, one campaign template for brands, both runnable with the content team and existing product surfaces. I say no to anything needing engineering beyond a landing page in the first quarter. Bare bones is the constraint and the design principle.

15. "Why should we believe you will still be here, engaged, in month four?"
    Because the foundational phase is the part I actually do: at Voicing I had no team under me and no inherited playbook, and my last two years were building motions from nothing with founding teams. And I want the consumer side; I already market a band with my own money. The fastest proof: judge me on the first two launches.

## The "isn't that just sales?" answer (marketer's version)

"Sales converts demand into revenue against a quota. This seat builds a distribution channel: I am buying reach with product access and co-marketing instead of cash, and the output is measured in users, activations and cohort quality, not bookings. There is commercial mechanics in it, negotiating the give and the get, and my deal background helps there. But the deliverable of a partnership here is a launch that brings 20,000 people in 14 days at near-zero CAC, and that is a growth channel, run like one: pipeline in, campaigns out, cohorts measured, losers cut."
